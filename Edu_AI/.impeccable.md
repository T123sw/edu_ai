## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are teachers working inside an AI-assisted teaching studio. They use the interface while preparing lessons, reviewing classroom observations, selecting teaching materials, and generating structured outputs such as reports, lesson plans, and quizzes. The core job is to stay oriented while moving between chat, source materials, and generated artifacts without losing context.

### Brand Personality
Calm, competent, supportive. The interface should feel like a reliable academic workbench rather than a flashy AI toy. It should reduce ambiguity, help users trust what the system is doing, and make the next step obvious.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light mode first. Editorial and workshop-like rather than futuristic. Warm neutrals, paper-like surfaces, restrained accent colors, and clear hierarchy should signal "serious teaching work in progress". Avoid neon, glossy dark themes, generic AI gradients, and over-produced dashboard chrome.

### Design Principles
1. Make system state legible before making it beautiful.
2. Prefer warm, grounded surfaces over synthetic AI styling.
3. Keep the chat workspace focused: short summaries, visible sources, and clear next actions.
4. Support long-form teaching work with calm hierarchy and low cognitive noise.
5. Introduce personality through typography, spacing, and subtle accents, not gimmicks.
